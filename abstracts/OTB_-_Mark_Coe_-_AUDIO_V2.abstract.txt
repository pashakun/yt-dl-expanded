PART 1 — ABSTRACT

This discussion centers on the evolution of digital infrastructure investments, particularly the transformation of public blockchain and Bitcoin mining companies amidst exploding AI and high-performance computing (HPC) demand. It examines how investor and regulatory climates have shifted, catalyzing both opportunities and risks for institutional players adapting to new business models, such as Bitcoin miners pivoting to data center operations. The stakes are high for both the sustainability of crypto-exposed business models and the resilience and profitability of US-based mining, especially as AI infrastructure demands absorb available power and regulatory clarity encourages broader institutional participation. Unresolved questions persist regarding the long-term viability of digital asset treasury companies, the ultimate fate of US mining profitability post-2028 halving, and whether public market structures and counterparties in this new landscape are built on sustainable fundamentals or at risk of collapse when market conditions turn.


PART 2 — KEY IDEAS

- There is a growing convergence between traditional financial businesses and blockchain/crypto infrastructure, expanding the opportunity set in public markets.
- Regulatory clarity, especially in the US, has unlocked significant institutional and capital markets activity, facilitating more IPOs and reducing prior legal uncertainties.
- The proliferation of public “digital asset treasury” companies—entities primarily holding crypto assets—has generated arbitrage opportunities but raises concerns about sustainability and NAV discounts during downturns.
- Bitcoin mining has become increasingly uneconomic in the US due to capital intensity, rising global competition from nation-states, and the BTC halving cycle, with miners massively underperforming Bitcoin returns.
- Many public Bitcoin miners are pivoting towards AI and HPC data center operations, leveraging their power contracts and locations to meet strong new demand—but this transition is capital-intensive and operationally complex.
- As AI/HPC demand outstrips grid capacity, access to abundant, reliable power becomes the primary differentiator, rather than any legacy Bitcoin mining expertise.
- There are significant risks regarding business model transitions, including management ability, infrastructure financing, and counterparty risk—especially considering multi-year contract mismatches between lessees and tenants in AI/HPC deals.
- Alternative and renewable energy sources (not just nuclear, but also natural gas, wind, and solar) are under consideration to meet rising infrastructure demands, but timelines and reliability remain challenges.
- The future of US-based public Bitcoin mining appears bleak beyond 2028 as profitability is likely to collapse, with only a few operations remaining as adjuncts to other businesses or being mothballed altogether.
- The institutional market structure is expected to further blend crypto-native and traditional finance infrastructure, potentially enabling more sophisticated hedging and trading strategies as these systems converge.

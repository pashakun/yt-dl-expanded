PART 1 — ABSTRACT

This conversation centers on the evolution and design philosophy of institutional blockchain infrastructure, focusing on the Canton network developed by Digital Asset. The discussion explores the nuanced debate between permissioned and permissionless networks, emphasizing the challenges of privacy, composability, and incentive alignment when building blockchain platforms for traditional finance (TradFi) institutions. The founders argue that real-world adoption depends on privacy by default, practical interoperation between use cases, and incentive structures that reward builders and users, not just validators. Crucially, unresolved are questions about how far established financial institutions will go in adopting on-chain processes, whether new privacy models will undermine existing transparency features valued in DeFi, and how well ecosystem-driven tokenomics can overcome the cold start and network effects required for true institutional-scale change.

PART 2 — KEY IDEAS

- The public vs private blockchain distinction is often oversimplified; most "public" chains carry implicit permissioning through validator requirements or foundation control, while true institutional adoption hinges less on publicness than on privacy and control over data.
- Traditional financial market structures are highly inefficient in collateral utilization and settlement speed; on-chain native records with high-velocity movement and privacy could unlock vast new efficiencies and market structures.
- Privacy is non-negotiable for institutional use cases and is increasingly valued by sophisticated crypto-native actors, as transparency exposes sensitive information and may facilitate front-running or market manipulation.
- Zero knowledge proofs are distinguished from privacy; ZK provides anonymity but can introduce new risks (e.g., system fragility if collateral is not actually present), whereas privacy should mean limiting transaction visibility strictly to stakeholders.
- Composability—allowing applications with different privacy models and functional needs to interact atomically on the same network—is essential for building complex institutional workflows that do not sacrifice control or compliance.
- Real-world uptake among institutions is catalyzed by focusing on tangible business outcomes like collateral efficiency and cost savings, rather than evangelizing blockchain technology per se.
- The Canton network reflects a federated, interoperable architecture inspired by federations like Switzerland: distinct "Cantons" (application domains) interoperate, allowing specialization without loss of network effects.
- Network incentives are structured differently from other chains; a majority of newly minted token rewards go to application builders and active users, while miners/validators receive a smaller share, aiming to foster sustainable ecosystem growth rather than speculative hype.
- There is tension and uncertainty around whether large-scale institutional adoption will materialize, how privacy will interact with compliance demands, and whether new incentive systems can effectively bootstrap a diverse, interconnected ecosystem.
- The trajectory of private and institutional blockchains may mirror the transition from early intranets to the modern, heterogeneous Internet—suggesting that broader, interoperable networks with differentiated permissions and privacy may ultimately prevail.

## Abstract

PART 1 — ABSTRACT

This conversation explores emerging and persistent trends in crypto, such as stablecoins, metaverse applications, tokenization, gaming, the creator economy, meme coins, and the growing intersection of AI with blockchain. The panelists assess each sector’s potential over the upcoming year, drawing on their experiences as founders and operators. The discussion highlights the underlying issues shaping crypto’s trajectory: driving real-world utility, expanding community involvement, and confronting the fragmentation of token ecosystems. Key unresolved tensions include how to move beyond speculation to practical use cases, whether community-driven innovation can outpace institutional adaptation, and how consolidation—through mergers, acquisitions, or token mergers—will reshape markets. Broader questions persist around regulatory uncertainty, the concentration of liquidity, and the sustainability of current hype cycles versus long-term value creation.

PART 2 — KEY IDEAS

- Stablecoins: Considered among the strongest candidates for mass adoption due to fast, cheap, and simple global payments that easily onboard non-crypto users.
- Metaverse: Growth is driven by user-generated content, AI-powered no-code tools, and brand partnerships; however, general excitement is tempered by questions over sustained engagement and practical value.
- Creator Economy: Web3 promises to empower creators by reducing platform tax and intermediaries, but success depends on building easy-to-use tools and clear monetary incentives.
- Meme Coins: Despite originating as cultural phenomena, meme coins are viewed as key onramps for new crypto users and as expressions of organic community power, provoking tension with those focused on utility.
- AI & Crypto: The collision of AI with crypto is anticipated to unlock new automation and programmable finance use cases, but also raises new risks—such as autonomous agents wielding financial resources with unclear oversight.
- Gaming & Blockchain: Blockchain could help reinvent a struggling game industry by aligning incentives, redistributing value, and supporting new creator-driven models, but mainstream adoption remains limited and faces economic and regulatory headwinds.
- NFTs: Seen more as a foundational technology (“store of culture”) than a single product; potential exists in unexplored use cases and interoperability, but current sentiment is affected by weak market performance.
- Token Ecosystem Fragmentation: There are too many tokens and chains, leading to liquidity dilution and developer distraction; expectations are rising for token and protocol mergers (not just company M&A) to consolidate usage and align interests.
- Institutional Dynamics: VC money overwhelmingly favors infrastructure over applications, raising sustainability questions for consumer-facing projects and indicating possible over-supply of chains relative to active dApps.
- Regional Shifts: Asia (especially Hong Kong) and the Middle East (Dubai) are perceived as leading crypto hubs, while regulatory hostility in the US dampens sentiment about its role in the industry's near-term future.

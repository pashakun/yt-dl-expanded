## Abstract

PART 1 — ABSTRACT

This conversation centers on Franklin Templeton’s pioneering efforts to bridge traditional finance (TradFi) and decentralized finance (DeFi) by tokenizing mutual funds and other investment products on blockchain infrastructure. The discussion highlights both the operational efficiencies and new financial functionalities unlocked by on-chain architectures, such as instant settlement, granular yield calculations, and peer-to-peer transferability, while emphasizing the importance of security, regulatory clarity, and robust identity frameworks. This evolution is significant because it signals a broader institutional shift toward blockchain-based systems that could fundamentally reshape how capital markets, asset management, and payments operate globally. Yet, tensions remain around regulatory uncertainty, the technical challenges of migrating legacy systems, how quickly adoption will occur, and the balance between openness and compliance in integrating crypto-native and institutionally regulated products. Key questions about wallet adoption, composability, and the interplay between permissioned and permissionless systems are left unresolved, pointing to a complex transition phase for finance.

PART 2 — KEY IDEAS

- Tokenizing mutual funds on-chain yields significant operational savings, increased accuracy (minute-level recordkeeping), and new capabilities such as daily—and even intraday—yield payments and peer-to-peer transfer, which are unfeasible in traditional setups.
- Institutional adoption is accelerating as blockchain ecosystems mature, with infrastructure improvements like KYC-enabled wallets, whitelisting, and better cross-chain functionality facilitating engagement by major asset managers.
- Security and decentralization remain critical considerations for institutions; different blockchains are chosen based on client/order needs, balancing cost, speed, and perceived network trustworthiness.
- The main regulatory questions involve stablecoin yield restrictions, rules for KYC/AML compliance in DeFi liquidity pools, and frameworks for digital identity and smart contract enforceability—these are all unresolved but actively monitored.
- Real-world assets and cultural/intellectual property (e.g., art, collectibles) are expected to become mainstream investable asset classes via tokenization, expanding beyond traditional equities and fixed income.
- Widespread migration to on-chain funds is forecasted: all new fund launches are likely to be on-chain within five years, while legacy funds will migrate more slowly due to technological and administrative inertia.
- Wallet-based systems—enabling interoperability and composability of assets—are viewed as the linchpin for user adoption and new financial products, with wallet integration advancing through neobrokers, payments firms, and ETFs.
- Regulatory pace is seen as slower than the industry would prefer, but institutional actors and crypto natives are collaboratively shaping the evolving rules through ongoing dialogue.
- Permissioned DeFi (lending, liquidity pools) is a key area of interest; Franklin Templeton is prepared to participate more fully as soon as regulatory clarity emerges, especially regarding KYC and the treatment of registered products in DeFi.
- Ultimately, the firm envisions a future where blockchain rails are ubiquitous in finance—analogous to how the internet is now foundational to all business—rendering the distinction between “digital” and “traditional” assets obsolete.

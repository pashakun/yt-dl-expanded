## Abstract

PART 1 — ABSTRACT

This conversation centers on Noble, a Cosmos-based blockchain purpose-built to provide neutral infrastructure for the issuance and transfer of stablecoins, and its evolving role in the rapidly maturing crypto payment ecosystem. The discussion highlights how advancements in both legislation (e.g., the "genius" law) and blockchain infrastructure have transformed stablecoins from speculative assets to credible instruments for mainstream payments, potentially disrupting legacy financial rails. Noble’s strategy involves integrating composable, programmable yield and launching an Ethereum-compatible app layer, positioning itself at the intersection of institutional adoption and technical innovation. However, tensions remain regarding sustainable value capture for infrastructure providers versus applications, regulatory clarity around yield, and whether ecosystems like Cosmos can maintain relevance amidst shifting developer and liquidity dynamics. The ultimate impact on market structure and financial system integration is still uncertain, with open questions about long-term differentiation, composability, and governance models.

PART 2 — KEY IDEAS

- Stablecoins are poised to be the catalyst for making blockchain the backbone of mainstream financial payments, with recent legislation enabling legal frameworks and adoption by legacy players.
- The "genius" law is identified as a fundamental regulatory shift, legitimizing stablecoins as payment instruments and opening the door for both traditional and crypto-native institutions to innovate in payments.
- Institutional interest in crypto, particularly stablecoins, is rising sharply, driven by yield opportunities and improving infrastructure, but not all sources of yield are equal or sustainable.
- Yield distribution for stablecoins is governed by evolving legal constraints; typically, issuers cannot directly pass through yield, but partners (like exchanges or DeFi protocols) can distribute it within compliance.
- Noble positions itself as neutral infrastructure, focusing on routing and scalability rather than issuing or branding stablecoins, with composable yield allowing customization for distribution partners.
- Despite robust technology (Cosmos stack), native token value accrual remains a structural problem; adoption does not guarantee tokenholder upside, raising broader questions about value capture in modular blockchain architectures.
- Cosmos is described as persistent and relevant due to its technical stack, even as native tokens underperform and liquidity concentrates elsewhere; the analogy is made to Linux as ubiquitous but not always directly monetized.
- Noble is launching an EVM-compatible L2 secured by its POA layer to gain developer mindshare and performance advantages, rather than relying on less well-maintained Cosmos EVM incarnations.
- The design choice of a proof-of-authority (POA) security model for the L2 is based on the belief it best serves stablecoin use cases, but could trade off broader economic security and decentralized control.
- There are still unresolved questions about the long-term sustainability of stablecoin issuance, integration with traditional finance, value capture for infrastructure providers, and the routes for composable yield amid regulatory uncertainty.

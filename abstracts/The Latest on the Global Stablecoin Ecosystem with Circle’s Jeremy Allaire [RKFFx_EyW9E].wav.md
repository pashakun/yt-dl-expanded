## Abstract

PART 1 — ABSTRACT

This discussion centers on the evolving global stablecoin ecosystem, with a focus on Circle's USDC and its role in building the foundational infrastructure of an "internet financial system." The conversation addresses how stablecoins, regulatory frameworks, and cross-chain technologies are reshaping the relationship between crypto, traditional finance, and sovereign monetary systems. This matters for the future of finance as stablecoins could enable seamless, programmable, and borderless money, fundamentally altering economic participation, institutional practices, and the balance of power in monetary policy. However, unresolved issues persist around regulatory harmonization, the risk of dollarization for emerging markets, competition between centralized and decentralized models, and the ultimate shape of regulation for novel financial products built atop or alongside stablecoins.

PART 2 — KEY IDEAS

- There is rapid global expansion and increasing market share of USDC, with Circle building local banking rails across numerous regions to streamline fiat on/off ramps and redemption.
- Regulatory clarity and the legal definition of stablecoins are seen as essential for mainstream adoption and integration into real economic activity, not just speculative trading.
- Stablecoins are likely to become foundational infrastructure for an "internet financial system," potentially increasing global economic output and transforming credit and settlement practices.
- The proliferation of blockchain protocols will likely continue, with a layered model (e.g., app-chains, layer-threes) enabling customizability for enterprises and geographic scaling.
- Dollar-denominated stablecoins currently dominate, raising policy concerns for non-U.S. governments about accelerated dollarization and loss of monetary control, especially in emerging markets.
- The widespread utility and irresistible value proposition of internet-native money may create tensions between user demand and local regulatory/policy objectives.
- In the long run, regulated, fully-reserved, centralized issuers are expected to provide stablecoins suitable for institutions and real-world commerce, while algorithmic and synthetic stable tokens will remain niche, primarily for trading and risk management.
- Regulatory treatment of synthetic and derivative-based stablecoins remains uncertain, with questions about their classification and future accessibility.
- Systemic risks remain in the crypto sector, notably that catastrophic failures at key firms or protocols could undermine trust and impede institutional adoption.
- There is skepticism that "code is speech" alone will prevent the regulation of financial (and other) algorithms—jurisdictions will likely impose oversight regardless of technical structure.

## Abstract

PART 1 — ABSTRACT

This conversation examines the evolving landscape of crypto custody, focusing on the debate between self-custody and institutional custodians, as well as the increasing importance of staking as a service. The discussion highlights why the custody layer remains complex and non-commoditized in crypto, unlike in traditional finance, given ongoing technological and regulatory change and the growth of programmability and on-chain services. These issues matter as institutional adoption and regulatory clarity will shape whether crypto infrastructure supports secure, scalable participation across new financial ecosystems—or leaves significant risk unresolved. Unanswered questions persist around whether large institutions can keep up with innovation, whether regulatory harmonization is possible, and how the industry will balance user control (self-custody) with the compliance demands and risk management practices of regulated custodians.

PART 2 — KEY IDEAS

- Crypto custody is much less commoditized than in traditional finance, due to rapid changes in blockchain protocols, asset standards, and user needs; custodians must adapt constantly.
- Secure custody is non-negotiable: a single security failure can destroy a custodian's business, making ongoing investment in defense and operational discipline essential.
- The programmability of blockchain assets means that many functions (e.g., staking) can be performed directly on-chain, reducing reliance on intermediaries and challenging traditional business models.
- Staking as a service is a growing and profitable area for custodians, but differs fundamentally when done via custodians (with segregated on-chain wallets) versus centralized exchanges (where assets are pooled and users may become mere creditors).
- Regulatory treatment of staking by exchanges may shift, as such offerings resemble collective investment schemes and trigger additional oversight concerns.
- Building a custody solution is technologically complex due to the diversity of blockchain protocols, bridging requirements, and emerging DeFi/Web3 integrations—which large incumbents may struggle to address quickly.
- Regulations for custody are inconsistent worldwide; some countries regulate only exchanges or have minimal oversight, complicating operations for global custodians.
- Traditional financial institutions moving into crypto custody face major frictions related to the pace of technological development and decision-making inflexibility, making partnerships and acquisitions more viable than in-house builds.
- Regulatory fragmentation both creates barriers to entry and hinders customer protection; most custodians pursue compliance to a high, common denominator standard to operate globally, which may sacrifice competitiveness in some local markets.
- The future of custody will likely involve a mix of regulated custody providers, self-custody, and hybrid/shared custody models, with potential convergence as legal, technical, and compliance solutions evolve.

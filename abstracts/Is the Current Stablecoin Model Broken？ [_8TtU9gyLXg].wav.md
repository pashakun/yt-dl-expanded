## Abstract

PART 1 — ABSTRACT

This conversation examines the limitations of current stablecoin models and explores the potential evolution towards a system that offers greater transparency and distributes yield to users rather than centralized issuers. The discussion highlights the mounting regulatory complexities, particularly with proposed U.S. laws such as the Genius Act that enforce strict rules on asset backing and yield distribution, and suggests a likely future bifurcation between U.S.-compliant “utility” stablecoins and higher-yield products accessible elsewhere. At its core, this debate matters because stablecoins are foundational to crypto’s infrastructure and global money flows, yet their continued growth hinges on trust, regulatory clarity, and aligning incentives for users versus issuers. Persistent tensions remain over regulatory constraints, jurisdictional fragmentation, and whether efforts to ensure compliance could stifle rather than foster innovation in financial infrastructure.

PART 2 — KEY IDEAS

- Persistent doubts about the legitimacy of asset backing and lack of user access to yield undermine trust in current (1.0) stablecoins.
- Stablecoin 2.0 concepts propose splitting principal and yield, using transparent on-chain collateralization (e.g., tokenized treasuries) to address opacity concerns.
- There is strong demand for user participation in stablecoin yield, but regulatory treatment of yield-bearing tokens often classifies them as securities, creating legal and usability hurdles.
- U.S. regulation, such as the Genius Act, will likely drive a split between compliant, lower-yield stablecoins and more lucrative, less regulated offerings for global markets.
- Large companies and ecosystems may issue their own branded stablecoins, tailoring collateral and yields to local regulatory regimes and business needs.
- The regulatory landscape is deeply shaped by lobbying from traditional banks, which see user yield as an existential threat to their business models.
- Future stablecoin interoperability may rely on clearing houses or interchanges to ensure fungibility and efficient transfers among varied regulatory “flavors.”
- AI is forecasted to further streamline crypto transactions, abstracting away complexity for users and automatically optimizing transfers across stablecoin types and networks.
- The underlying tension remains: regulatory attempts to constrain stablecoin innovation may slow adoption in certain regions, while technological workarounds and global demand continue to drive evolution beyond regulatory borders.

# Will Every Company Have Its Own Stablecoin? Yes, Says Stripe's Bridge

## Abstract

PART 1 — ABSTRACT

This conversation centers on the idea that, as the stablecoin sector evolves, every major fintech, neobank, or platform may issue its own programmable stablecoin rather than rely on a handful of dominant issuers like Circle and Tether. This trend is seen as essential for aligning incentives, broadening use cases, and reducing systemic risk in digital finance, as well as unlocking new avenues for global payments, interoperability, and user empowerment. If realized, such a shift would make stablecoins core global money movement infrastructure and could foster financial innovation, cost savings, and more inclusive services. However, important questions remain around fragmentation, user experience (will users have to handle many tokens?), interoperability between diverse stablecoins, security and compliance for issuers, and the pace at which traditional banking models might be disrupted. The full implications for existing financial institutions, network effects, and future regulation are still unresolved.

PART 2 — KEY IDEAS

- The current stablecoin market is heavily concentrated, with two issuers (USDC/Circle and USDT/Tether) controlling most of the supply and limiting use cases by capturing yield and restricting programmability.
- Open issuance platforms, like Bridge (acquired by Stripe), aim to let any business or financial platform issue its own stablecoin, unlocking tailored incentives and new payment models.
- The ability for companies to control their own stablecoins enables greater flexibility (e.g., programmable rewards, tailored cross-border payments), risk management, and direct economic participation.
- One risk of this proliferation is fragmentation, but proponents argue that interoperability layers will allow seamless exchange and conversion between different stablecoins, making the user experience simple and abstracted.
- Stablecoins are likely to become “invisible infrastructure,” with users interacting with digital dollars without needing to know the issuer, while platforms handle conversion and settlement under the hood.
- Large-scale adoption depends on building robust, compliant custody, reserve management, and risk controls; engaging major banking partners (like BlackRock) is viewed as essential for these functions.
- Settlement and payment networks, like Visa, are starting to experiment with stablecoin rails, but efficiency and network effects require slow, incremental change as more players join.
- The evolution of purpose-built payment blockchains, such as Stripe’s Tempo, seeks to address deficiencies (cost, TPS limits, wallet creation issues) in current blockchains for high-frequency, global-scale financial activity.
- The new paradigm challenges incumbent banks, potentially accelerating their disruption as stablecoin models provide global, programmable, instantly-settling money while passing yield to users.
- The rise of programmable, AI-compatible money flows (with stablecoins as programmable cash, held by both people and machines) could lead to new payment, credit, and agent-driven economic models that remain to be fully explored or exploited.

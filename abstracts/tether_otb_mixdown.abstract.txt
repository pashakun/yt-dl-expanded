PART 1 — ABSTRACT

This conversation centers on Tether's unprecedented global growth as a USD-backed stablecoin, the evolving landscape of US stablecoin regulation, and Tether's efforts to achieve a Big Four audit. It unpacks the geopolitical dynamics of digital dollarization, the emergent competition from BRICS-backed and gold-backed stablecoins, and the strategic role of Tether’s international distribution network, especially in emerging markets where traditional financial infrastructure is limited. The discussion highlights regulatory frictions—particularly the bifurcation between domestic and international stablecoins, political resistance within the US, and the prior challenges of securing institutional audits due to regulatory pressure. Important tensions remain unresolved around stablecoin regulation models, the impact of dollarization on local economies, potential systemic risks of regulatory approaches like MiCA in the EU, and the disruptive role of interest-bearing stablecoins. 

PART 2 — KEY IDEAS

- Tether’s exponential supply growth reflects the demand for USD-denominated assets globally, particularly in emerging markets lacking stable local currencies or strong banking infrastructure.
- The upcoming wave of US stablecoin regulation is viewed by Tether as a national security issue, potentially solidifying the dollar’s influence and countering de-dollarization efforts led by BRICS and others.
- There is a push for regulatory differentiation between domestic, institutionally-focused stablecoins and foreign/international stablecoins targeting underserved populations—implying distinct legal and operational regimes.
- Political hostility towards crypto under past US administrations has hindered Tether’s ability to obtain a Big Four audit; a recent shift in regulatory climate is enabling progress on this front.
- Tether’s operations prioritize ground-level adoption in emerging markets, investing in physical distribution networks (e.g., energy kiosks, local stores) rather than competing for developed-market payments volume.
- The company expresses concern that some regulatory frameworks—such as MiCA’s reserve requirements—could inadvertently create systemic risks in traditional banking systems.
- Tether is actively moving key operations to El Salvador, citing its pragmatic digital asset legislation as a better fit for its business and mission than conventional financial centers in Europe or the US.
- The possibility of gold-backed or BRICS-backed stablecoins is seen as a real competitive threat in the next few years, with the expectation that such initiatives may launch by 2025-2026.
- Data collection and transparency initiatives are being ramped up to demonstrate Tether’s use cases beyond trading—targeting remittances, savings, and daily payments—with plans to release granular data analytics.
- Interest-bearing stablecoins present more of a challenge in developed markets; Tether’s prevailing view is that for unbanked populations, holding value in digital dollars supersedes the need for yield.
- There is acknowledged reputational and regulatory risk: large-scale crypto-dollarization could draw blame from governments for destabilizing local economies, even as Tether argues that fundamental economic problems precede such adoption.

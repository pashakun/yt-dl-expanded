PART 1 — ABSTRACT

This conversation centers on the development and potential impact of a GBP-denominated stablecoin by Agant, addressing the current dominance of USD stablecoins in both domestic UK and cross-border financial flows. The discussion explores how a trusted, compliant GBP stablecoin could modernize backend infrastructure for payments and settlements, offering efficiency gains and new opportunities to financial institutions and corporates constrained by legacy systems. The future significance lies in the ability for local currency stablecoins to foster innovation in financial products, democratize access to yield, and reshape competitive dynamics, especially for retail banks. However, unresolved issues persist around the pace and direction of UK regulatory clarity, potential competition or co-option by incumbent banks, the durability of business models amid regulatory shifts, and the challenge of catalyzing broad on-chain liquidity beyond a USD-centric environment.

PART 2 — KEY IDEAS

- The lack of a GBP stablecoin creates operational, FX, and settlement frictions for UK institutions operating in crypto, as most liquidity and products are USD-based.
- Adoption of non-USD stablecoins is hindered not by demand but by liquidity network effects and the lack of compliant, local options.
- Agant positions itself as a fintech leveraging blockchain rather than a traditional crypto firm, aiming to be trusted and compliant as a foundation-layer provider.
- Institutional demand for GBP stablecoins is rising, with asset managers, banks, and payment providers seeking solutions to reduce costs, complexity, and risk associated with cross-currency and cross-border flows.
- Regulatory frameworks in the UK lag behind other jurisdictions, with no dedicated stablecoin regulation yet, resulting in uncertainty and provisional use of existing AML/compliance regimes.
- UK regulators currently prohibit interest/yield to token holders, constraining certain DeFi business models and making net interest margin strategies vulnerable to regulatory changes.
- The competitive threat from incumbent banks is muted in the short term, as regulators are cautious about banks entering unregulated stablecoin markets until clear rules emerge.
- Local regulatory authorization and jurisdictional credibility are seen as essential to winning the confidence of institutions and forming compliant partnerships.
- Public, permissionless blockchains are favored for transparency and accessibility, but private/permissioned chains are relevant for specific institutional or internal uses.
- Retail banks face risk of disintermediation if fully-backed, programmable stablecoins reach scale, but may eventually adapt stablecoin rails as distribution channels or partners if unable to compete directly.

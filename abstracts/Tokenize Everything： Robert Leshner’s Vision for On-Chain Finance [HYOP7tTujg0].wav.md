## Abstract

PART 1 — ABSTRACT

This conversation centers on the accelerating trend of tokenizing real-world assets (RWAs) such as equities, bonds, and money markets for use in decentralized finance (DeFi) protocols. It explores why this movement is crucial for the future interplay between crypto and traditional financial institutions: bridging the current self-referential cycle of crypto-native assets to unlock broader systemic value and utility. The discussion highlights shifting regulatory climates, evolving institutional attitudes, and technical models for tokenization as key enablers, but also acknowledges unresolved questions about user demand, the coexistence of permissionless and permissioned assets, and DeFi’s future composability under compliance requirements. Persistent tensions remain around scalability, regulatory boundaries, and whether DeFi can maintain openness while integrating with legacy finance.

PART 2 — KEY IDEAS

- The growth ceiling for DeFi is limited until real-world, non-crypto-native assets are brought on-chain and made interoperable with DeFi protocols.
- Institutional and regulatory willingness, more than explicit legal changes, has been the recent catalyst for increased tokenization activity.
- Current demand for tokenized assets is mostly from crypto-native users and applications rather than traditional institutions, though the latter are increasingly open to interaction at the infrastructure level.
- Two tokenization models are emerging: “canonical” or native tokenization working closely with issuers (favored by institutions), and “wrapped” or synthetic models offering scalability (favored by retail and international markets).
- DeFi’s transformative potential for traditional finance lies in use cases enabled by programmability, fast settlement, and composability—features not fully achievable with traditional brokerages.
- Regulatory requirements mean some tokenized assets will require KYC or geographical restrictions, challenging the ideal of totally permissionless finance while allowing unified protocols to support both open and restricted assets.
- Institutional adoption at scale hinges on DeFi protocols enabling truly novel or substantially improved financial functionalities, not merely replicating existing offerings.
- DeFi’s own infrastructure may become an invisible backend for fintechs and traditional applications, particularly as stablecoins and other tokenized assets gain programmable utility.
- Decentralized governance, while ideologically important, has proven to slow execution; early-stage projects may benefit from centralized development before progressive decentralization.
- The future composability of both permissioned and permissionless assets within flexible DeFi protocols poses open technical and regulatory design questions.

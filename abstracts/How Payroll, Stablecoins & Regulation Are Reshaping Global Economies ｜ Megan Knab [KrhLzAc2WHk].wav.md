## Abstract

PART 1 — ABSTRACT

This conversation centers on how stablecoins, crypto-based payroll systems, and emerging regulation are reshaping the global financial infrastructure, with particular attention to their impact on both institutions and individuals. The discussion explores how legacy financial companies are adopting blockchain technology to remain competitive, and the potential for crypto-native solutions to reduce costs and complexity in global payroll and cross-border payments. Regulatory uncertainty—especially in the US—remains a key friction, with debates about the adequacy and implications of proposed legislation like the “Genius Act,” and concerns about whether regulation will support innovation or entrench legacy players. Unresolved tensions include the risk of institutional capture, the possibility of stifling innovation through regulation, the global dominance of USD-backed stablecoins, and ongoing questions about fair access and consumer protection within new financial models. The overarching theme is the fragile balance between technological potential, regulatory development, and the evolving role of established institutions in a rapidly changing landscape.

PART 2 — KEY IDEAS

- The entry of legacy payments giants (e.g., PayPal, Stripe) into stablecoins signals both their need to stay competitive and an inevitable disruption of their outdated systems, rather than a simple endorsement of crypto by established players.
- Stablecoins and blockchain-based payroll systems offer speed and cost advantages, promising to reduce reliance on intermediaries like payday lenders and cross-border payment processors, potentially saving billions annually.
- Regulatory clarity, such as that proposed in the Genius Act, could legitimize stablecoin issuers, set industry-wide standards for disclosures and audits, and enable more traditional institutions to participate, but final forms and enforcement remain uncertain.
- The Genius Act’s requirement for full reserve backing of stablecoins raises questions about consistency and fairness when banks operate under fractional reserve rules, highlighting regulatory double standards.
- There is a risk that big fintechs could “capture” stablecoin rails—replicating legacy frictions and high fees—and undermine the core peer-to-peer and open-access promise of crypto technology.
- Regulatory inertia in the US has pushed legal and compliance costs onto startups, stifling domestic innovation while unintentionally benefitting legal professionals; clear but balanced frameworks are strongly needed.
- Global adoption of crypto and stablecoins is accelerating, especially in emerging markets where they may leapfrog traditional infrastructure, but this also extends the reach and influence of USD-denominated assets.
- Stablecoins might function as a “Trojan horse” for US monetary dominance rather than a true break from the existing financial order, creating new geopolitical considerations.
- There are worries about programmable blockchains being used by governments as tools for control and exclusion, given the transparency and immutability of on-chain data.
- Broader financial inclusion remains a challenge; current US investor accreditation rules and the complexity of crypto onboarding restrict access to wealth-building opportunities for less affluent or less savvy individuals.

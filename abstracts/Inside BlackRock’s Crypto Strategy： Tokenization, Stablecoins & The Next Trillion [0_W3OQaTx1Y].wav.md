## Abstract

PART 1 — ABSTRACT

This conversation provides an inside look at BlackRock's evolving crypto strategy, with a keen focus on institutional adoption, tokenization, and the role of products like ETFs and stablecoins. The discussion examines why institutional participation is still early and incremental, the key factors that could unlock broader engagement—such as correlation with traditional assets and regulatory clarity—and the potential transformative impact of tokenizing a wide range of financial products. Central unresolved tensions include whether crypto assets like Bitcoin will truly behave as uncorrelated hedges, how regulatory and market infrastructure will respond, and whether tokenization will fulfill its broad ambitions or remain limited to domains like stablecoins. The stakes are high: how these issues resolve will influence not only the trajectory of crypto markets but also reshape the future of traditional finance and capital markets.

PART 2 — KEY IDEAS

- Institutional participation in crypto remains in its infancy, with most large investors allocating only 1-3% of portfolios and true widespread adoption still pending.
- The narrative that crypto cycles are dictated by events such as halvings or market liquidity is being challenged, as increased institutional involvement may dampen traditional boom-bust dynamics.
- Institutions view Bitcoin’s correlation to other assets (especially gold versus equities) as critical; if seen as an uncorrelated hedge, allocation could increase substantially.
- ETF adoption has been historically rapid, especially for Bitcoin, facilitating easier institutional access and signaling growing, though still minor, asset allocation.
- Tokenization holds promise for transforming capital markets by enabling real-time settlement, programmability, and greater access, but so far, meaningful utility has only emerged in money market funds and stablecoins.
- The "bull case" for tokenization is broad adoption across asset classes (stocks, bonds, real estate), but a "bear case" could see success limited mostly to stablecoins and related cash-management instruments.
- Regulatory clarity is advancing but remains a substantial hurdle, particularly regarding how traditional frameworks adapt to tokenized assets; progress is most likely to come from regulatory agencies rather than sweeping legislative action.
- Market infrastructure for custody has made significant progress, but liquidity venues—especially outside DeFi—are less mature, creating friction for broader institutional adoption.
- The main challenge for tokenization is “real-world utility”: until end investors see clear advantages over existing structures, inertia and operational risk will limit uptake.
- Even in the optimistic scenario, network effects suggest a few dominant stablecoins will likely capture most of the market, rather than a fragmented landscape.

PART 1 — ABSTRACT

This conversation centers on Predicate, a technology for embedding programmable compliance and verification rules into blockchain transactions, addressing challenges at the intersection of crypto innovation and financial regulation. As blockchains evolve into global financial settlement systems, the ability to enforce standards like KYC/AML, privacy requirements, and market-specific constraints programmatically is becoming crucial for institutional adoption and for bridging regulated and unregulated market participants. The discussion highlights how Predicate’s approach goes beyond rigid KYC to support nuanced, use-case-specific checks, potentially lowering barriers for inclusion and enabling composability across various finance applications. However, unresolved tensions include the risk of over-regulating or excluding marginalized users, the uncertain pace and shape of regulation (especially in major markets like the US), and the challenge of preserving both privacy and compliance in increasingly interconnected digital financial systems.

PART 2 — KEY IDEAS

- Predicate enables programmable, policy-driven verification of wallets and transactions, allowing financial products to embed compliance logic directly on-chain.
- Institutional adoption of crypto is gated by the need to operate seamlessly across regulated and unregulated markets, with Predicate providing critical infrastructure for navigating complex legal and compliance requirements.
- Demand arises most from larger, scaling projects and those dealing with real-world assets (RWAs), stablecoins, liquidity pools, and privacy—areas needing reliable, adaptable compliance gates.
- Predicate’s system allows for nuanced guardrails (beyond KYC), such as sanction screening, transaction volume limits, or market integrity controls, which can be tailored to varying regulatory and business needs.
- The technology facilitates future-oriented use cases, including AI agent guardrails and cross-chain compliance enforcement in tokenized financial markets.
- There is a predicted continuum: highly-regulated, policy-rich segments will coexist with unregulated or “gray” crypto markets, and Predicate aims to sit at the regulated intersection while remaining agnostic to chain or market.
- Cross-institutional policy networks (analogous to Web2 payment rails or financial infrastructure consortia) could reduce redundant checks and enable interoperability, but require standardization and institutional buy-in.
- Tensions remain between regulatory requirements (which often rely on KYC and identification) and the goals of financial inclusion, especially for underserved populations who lack formal documentation or access.
- Privacy-preserving compliance is seen as an unsolved challenge; widespread adoption and robust tooling for compliant privacy are still lacking.
- The timeline for mass institutional or securities-related crypto adoption hinges on the slow evolution of clear regulatory frameworks, with meaningful progress in the US expected over one to two years as both legislative and agency-level rules clarify.

PART 1 — ABSTRACT

This discussion centers on addressing the inefficiencies of global foreign exchange (FX) and cross-border payments infrastructure, particularly through the lens of crypto-derived innovations like stablecoins. Santi Alvarado, drawing on experience in Latin American fintech and crypto, argues that much of the world’s financial plumbing still relies on outdated models requiring costly capital pre-funding and opaque, analog processes. The conversation suggests that programmable stablecoins, coupled with a new layer of real-time institutional settlement infrastructure, could unlock major cost and speed improvements—especially for remittances and cross-border e-commerce. Why this matters is that such advancements may ultimately reshape how liquidity flows, dramatically reduce systemic friction, and level the playing field for smaller, less liquid markets. However, questions persist on whether stablecoin liquidity and trust are sufficient in less mature markets, and if incumbents will cede control to more transparent, programmable systems.

PART 2 — KEY IDEAS

- The global FX market is the world’s largest, yet remains inefficient, slow, and capital intensive due to legacy systems (e.g., Swift messaging and Nostro/Vostro accounts) that require $4 trillion in tied-up working capital.
- Stablecoins could provide a unified, programmable standard for both messaging and value transfer, potentially enabling true real-time cross-border payments with less need for pre-funded capital.
- Institutional users face specific frictions: lack of price transparency, slow settlement (often delayed by several days), and batch-based, manual liquidity balancing processes.
- XFX aims to solve these by offering real-time settlement, price consistency, automated auditability, and programmable APIs for both FX and stablecoin payouts—initially focusing on USD-MXN and stablecoin-MXN rails.
- The greatest immediate value for such infrastructure appears in “exotic” or less liquid emerging markets, where inefficient incumbent systems are most costly.
- Future expansion hinges on overcoming current stablecoin liquidity constraints in many regions, as well as demonstrating institutional-grade spread consistency and depth across multiple currency pairs.
- Even with improved rails, cross-border money transmitters and payment processors must manage risk if end-market liquidity or stablecoin acceptance is insufficient.
- The shift to programmatic, API-driven FX and settlements threatens the business models of incumbent banks by unbundling key proprietary infrastructure.
- Regulatory clarity and stablecoin risk profiles remain unresolved for maximizing institutional adoption.
- The competitive edge hinges on abstracting away the choice of stablecoin, routing transactions via the path with best liquidity and lowest cost, rather than allegiance to any one token.

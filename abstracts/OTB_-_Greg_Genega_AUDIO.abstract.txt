PART 1 — ABSTRACT

This conversation centers on KPMG’s evolving role in crypto industry growth, exploring how the firm adapts its audit, advisory, and compliance services to meet the unique needs of digital asset companies and traditional financial institutions engaging with crypto. The discussion highlights the increasing institutionalization of crypto, particularly with new standards for stablecoins, real-world asset tokenization, and greater regulatory scrutiny. The interplay between public and permissioned blockchain systems, the challenges of demonstrating asset existence and internal controls, and the lagging but growing adoption among traditional firms reflect deeper tensions about risk, compliance, technological adaptation, and the ultimate value proposition of blockchain infrastructure. Key unresolved questions include the pace and direction of regulatory clarity, how traditional institutions will ultimately embrace or resist crypto integration, and ongoing challenges in merging legacy processes with novel, fast-evolving digital asset frameworks.

PART 2 — KEY IDEAS

- KPMG has been active in crypto since 2017, pioneering digital asset audits, advising on major M&A, and supporting clients ranging from DAOs to major ETF issuers, stablecoin operators, and blockchain infrastructure firms.
- The firm invests heavily in proprietary tools (e.g., Chain Fusion) to facilitate on-chain data audits, reflecting the technical demands and complexities unique to digital assets.
- Attestations and assurance standards are rapidly evolving, with regulations like the pending Genius Act potentially mandating monthly attestations for stablecoins, driving new service offerings.
- Distinctions between “audit” as defined in public accounting versus “audits” in crypto (such as smart contract reviews) create friction in expectations and scope with industry clients.
- Permissionless (public) blockchains are viewed as fundamentally more valuable for censorship resistance, but permissioned chains remain important for institutional adoption, often due to regulatory or operational requirements.
- Audit approaches differ between public and permissioned chains, but both require robust processes for verifying asset existence, rights, and internal controls, especially when third parties lack standardized assurances.
- The conflation and confusion between blockchain, Bitcoin, stablecoins, and crypto persists among traditional firms, slowing mainstream adoption, though education and regulatory progress are improving attitudes post-FTX.
- Real-world asset tokenization and stablecoin integration are identified as potential catalysts for broader institutional adoption, but most traditional firms remain cautious, awaiting more legislative clarity.
- Evolving compliance needs—especially for KYC/AML and control systems in token transfers—are being addressed through protocol innovations, blending on-chain transparency with regulatory requirements.
- Persistent operational risks, such as overreliance on spreadsheets and weak documentation or custody practices, remain significant hurdles for crypto businesses pursuing scalable, institution-grade operations.

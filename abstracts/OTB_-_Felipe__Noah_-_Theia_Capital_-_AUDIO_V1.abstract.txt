PART 1 — ABSTRACT

This conversation centers on the evolving landscape of crypto investment, especially from the perspective of liquid token funds versus the dominant venture capital model in the industry. Felipe and Noah discuss how institutional capital, regulatory shifts, and rapid market changes are reshaping both the opportunities and challenges for crypto funds that take a fundamentally-driven, long-term approach. The dialogue underscores the tension between narrative-driven speculation and sustainable value creation linked to real cash flows, as well as the risks of over-venturization and high attrition among liquid funds. Unresolved questions remain regarding whether crypto-native projects or traditional fintechs will capture the next wave of on-chain financial adoption, how regulatory clarity will shape investor protections and business models, and whether the sector can attract and retain top, mission-driven talent necessary for long-term innovation.

PART 2 — KEY IDEAS

- The panel argues most token prices are currently overvalued relative to fundamentals, with market cycles driven more by flows and narratives than by sustainable business metrics.
- Regulatory developments in the US are seen as a material tailwind, likely to enable greater institutional adoption and mainstream on-chain finance, but the precise trajectories and beneficiaries remain uncertain.
- There is acute skepticism about exit strategies based solely on “greater fool” liquidity or retail entry; sustainable success is framed as ultimately dependent on real users generating revenue and cash flow.
- Liquid crypto funds face structural disadvantages compared to venture funds, notably much higher attrition rates, risk of “blowing up” from leverage, and less favorable capital-raising optics for allocators seeking smoother mark-to-market profiles.
- The industry’s talent pipeline is viewed as insufficient and temporarily degraded by speculative frenzies (e.g., meme coins), compounded by stigma and competition from other hot sectors such as AI.
- Long-term outperformance is believed to lie in concentrated, fundamentals-driven strategies investing in durable teams/businesses, rather than trading flows or chasing volatility.
- The use of LLMs and programmatic analysis is described as a major operational edge for small, nimble funds, drastically altering traditional research and modeling workflows, though adoption is expected to be slow among large, path-dependent financiers.
- There is concern that many crypto-native projects may not benefit directly from on-chain adoption if they fail to serve broader user needs or match the work ethic and professionalism of incoming traditional fintech competitors.
- On the investment side, lending and credit-focused DeFi protocols are highlighted as more attractive and scalable, supported by real-world assets coming on-chain, compared to volume-based protocols whose metrics may be inflated by ephemeral trading activity.
- Futarchy—market-based governance exemplified by MetaDAO—is seen as a promising solution to tokenholder protections and agency problems, potentially serving as a crypto-native analog to the board/fiduciary system in traditional equity markets.

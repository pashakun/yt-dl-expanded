## Abstract

PART 1 — ABSTRACT

This conversation centers on Saudi Arabia’s unprecedented initiative to place its entire national real estate registry on a blockchain, aiming to tokenize and eventually fractionalize all property and land titles in the country. The project is framed as a landmark in both real estate and blockchain adoption, promising enhanced security, transparency, and liquidity for property transactions, with the potential to transform how assets are managed and traded. Its significance arises from the scale of implementation, regulatory alignment, and potential to serve as a model for other nations, especially in rapidly digitizing regions. Yet, unresolved questions include whether such tokenization will produce effective, liquid marketplaces, how global regulatory standards will evolve, and whether technological and institutional readiness elsewhere can match Saudi Arabia’s unique environment.

PART 2 — KEY IDEAS

- Tokenizing Saudi Arabia’s real estate registry is seen as a global first, aiming for full national coverage and delivery by 2026.
- Asset tokenization is expected to lower transaction friction, enable fractional ownership, and grant wider access to real estate investment.
- Agility in regulatory leadership, national digitization strategies, and robust technology are highlighted as critical enablers for large-scale projects; these conditions may not be easily replicated elsewhere.
- Liquidity remains a historic challenge for tokenized assets; this project addresses it through the planned development of integrated marketplaces and open data access for proptech firms.
- The Middle East, given shared regulatory and technological ambitions, is predicted to lead in similar national-scale tokenization projects, with global adoption likely to be gradual.
- The real estate use case is viewed as especially promising, but other asset classes—such as bonds, stablecoins, and commodities—are also emerging candidates for tokenization.
- Key risks include use of unproven technology, the lack of coordinated regulatory oversight, and ecosystem fragmentation, all of which could undermine platform adoption or stability.
- Technological scalability is described as effectively solved; the bigger hurdles are regulatory, institutional, and market-related rather than purely technical.
- Institutional partnerships and regulatory involvement from the outset are positioned as crucial to both user protection and ecosystem success.
- Visionary leadership and national alignment (as seen in Saudi Arabia’s Vision 2030) are deemed decisive for transformative projects, but may be difficult to duplicate elsewhere.

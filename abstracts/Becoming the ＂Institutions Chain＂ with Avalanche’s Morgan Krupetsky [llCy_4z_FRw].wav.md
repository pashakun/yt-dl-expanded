## Abstract

PART 1 — ABSTRACT

This conversation centers on Avalanche’s approach to becoming the primary blockchain platform for institutional adoption and real-world asset tokenization. It explores how Avalanche’s architecture accommodates diverse enterprise needs by combining permissioned features, customizability, and native interoperability, thereby bridging traditional finance with emerging crypto rails. The discussion underscores the importance of real-world use cases (such as tokenized money market funds, equities, and private credit) for financial institutions and fintechs, while also touching on the frictions of integrating blockchain solutions into legacy systems and reaching global, often underbanked, end-users. Key unresolved challenges remain around mainstream user experience, liquidity fragmentation across chains, privacy at scale, and how multi-chain dynamics will ultimately shape the competitive landscape for institutional-grade blockchain platforms.

PART 2 — KEY IDEAS

- Avalanche leverages a modular, customizable L1 architecture that meets the varied regulatory, technical, and compliance requirements of institutions, including granular permissioning at the validator, transaction, and smart contract levels.
- Institutional demand is shifting from permissioned private blockchains (e.g., Hyperledger) toward public or hybrid permissionless networks, with Avalanche seeking to blend control and public-market benefits.
- Native interoperability between Avalanche subnets (L1s) reduces the need for external bridges and third-party trust, mitigating trust and security risks commonly associated with blockchain interoperability.
- Major real-world asset (RWA) use cases on Avalanche include tokenized money market funds, stablecoins, equities, and private credit, targeting both crypto-native and traditional finance stakeholders.
- Money market fund tokenization is gaining traction as a “digital savings account,” with practical integrations into neobanks and fintechs, especially in emerging markets, offering instant yield and liquidity.
- Blockchain’s programmability enables operational efficiencies in private credit (e.g., automating loan reconciliation and facility draws), lowering entry barriers for smaller market players.
- UX/UI and usability remain significant hurdles for mainstream adoption, with an industry-wide need to abstract blockchain complexity from end-users and institutional clients.
- Liquidity fragmentation across networks and subnets is a risk; Avalanche’s strategy relies on seamless interoperability and UI abstraction to minimize friction and unify liquidity.
- The proliferation of stablecoins and the convergence of DeFi, TradFi, and tokenized assets are seen as primary growth vectors, driving both institutional adoption and embedded finance use cases across sectors.
- The competitive future of blockchain for institutions is expected to be multi-chain, but Avalanche positions itself as a leading platform for turnkey, customizable chains for both financial and non-financial real-world applications.

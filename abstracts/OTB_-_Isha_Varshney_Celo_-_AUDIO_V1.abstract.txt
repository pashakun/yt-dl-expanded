PART 1 — ABSTRACT

This discussion centers on the adoption of digital money and stablecoins in Sub-Saharan Africa, using Kenya’s M-Pesa as a launch point to explore the region’s transition from physical cash to mobile and, increasingly, on-chain digital assets. The conversation highlights why stablecoins—especially dollar-based—are vital in economies where local currencies are volatile and banking infrastructure is either limited or untrusted, and investigates the real-world frictions and user experiences that shape what “financial inclusion” can look like. The dialogue underscores noteworthy applications (such as remittances, micro-loans, and business-to-business settlements) but also exposes unresolved tensions, such as regulatory risks, the challenge of liquidity for non-dollar stablecoins, and how decentralized governance can or should evolve in new digital financial ecosystems. Questions remain around scaling liquidity, the social and technical mechanisms of onboarding (like on/off ramps or local kiosks), product-market fit for non-USD stablecoins, and the long-term role of internet-native tokens in practical finance.

PART 2 — KEY IDEAS

- M-Pesa’s dominance in Kenya demonstrates that digitizing money—first via mobile, now increasingly on-chain—radically increases access and unlocks new financial services, despite starting from a context with little traditional banking.
- Stablecoins, particularly those pegged to the US dollar, have become a store of value and transactional medium in regions with volatile local currencies, filling gaps left by both banks and previous mobile money systems.
- Real-world on/off ramps (physical kiosks, agent networks) have proven essential for mass adoption of mobile money, but as behavior shifts toward digital, reliance on such infrastructure may diminish over time—albeit at varying rates by country.
- Non-USD stablecoins have not reached scale, primarily due to thin liquidity and weak demand, though there's a growing interest in local-currency tokens, especially for use cases like FX and lending, contingent on deeper liquidity and institutional buy-in.
- Remittances are cited as a killer use case for on-chain stablecoins, with dramatic improvements in speed and cost over traditional channels, and evidence of rapid adoption through integrated platforms like Opera MiniPay.
- The fragmentation of products (retail wallets vs. business settlement vs. purpose-built remittance platforms) mirrors fragmentation of user needs; trust, established brands, and integration with local habits and behaviors are crucial in shifting user behavior.
- Regulatory risk is present but currently limited due to the structure of ecosystems (e.g., Celo as a platform, not a direct issuer), though the space can expect more scrutiny as stablecoin use grows.
- Institutional capital’s entry is seen as pivotal for building the liquidity necessary to support non-USD stablecoins and tokenized real-world assets, but adoption by retail and small businesses often precedes wholesale institutional involvement.
- Decentralized governance, enabled by native tokens, is viewed as necessary for credible neutrality and regional adaptation, though the proliferation of such tokens across blockchains may ultimately consolidate.
- Localized on-chain financial products (e.g., lending circles, payroll, gift cards, even music streaming with micro-subscriptions) are emerging, validating the space but also showing the importance of cultural adaptation over mere technological importation.

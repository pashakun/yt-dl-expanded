## Abstract

PART 1 — ABSTRACT

This conversation centers on the race among crypto networks to become the foundational infrastructure for traditional finance (TradFi), focusing on the intersection of tokenization, regulatory uncertainty, and institutional adoption. The participants examine the evolving role of blockchains like Ethereum, Canton, and Solana as platforms for tokenized assets, highlighting moves by major institutions (e.g., JPMorgan, BlackRock) and the nuanced competition between permissioned and public chains. The discussion matters because it signals a structural shift in how financial assets may be issued, traded, and settled, with implications for market efficiency, compliance, and access. However, tensions remain unresolved around regulatory clarity, the practical utility and liquidity of tokenized instruments, governance models for institutional blockchains, and whether public blockchains can match specialized networks in meeting institutional requirements while preserving open access.

PART 2 — KEY IDEAS

- Regulatory ambiguity, especially around taxonomy (security vs. commodity), remains a primary barrier to broader institutional participation in crypto markets.
- Large, established institutions are experimenting with tokenization, but most impactful progress could hinge on regulatory certainty and comfort with custody, settlement, and compliance.
- Competition between established intermediaries (e.g., Citadel) and new entrants creates political and commercial resistance to open market structure changes.
- The complexity and political infighting in US legislative processes likely delay any comprehensive market structure reform, pushing institutions to rely on regulator guidance or piecemeal progress.
- Regulatory approaches (e.g., "regulation by enforcement" under the Biden SEC) have generated skepticism and failed to prevent major crypto failures, with frustration about political polarization of crypto policy.
- Tokenized money market funds are seen as strategic for collateral mobility and operational efficiency, but their adoption to date is limited and target user segments (treasurers, liquidity providers, institutional investors) remain to be fully validated.
- The landscape of "institutional blockchains" is fragmented: Ethereum is valued for its history and neutrality, Canton aims to serve permissioned privacy-first institutional use cases, and Solana targets high-throughput, retail-oriented applications.
- The success of permissioned networks like Canton depends on their governance and willingness to scale access beyond initial participants; failures in previous financial consortiums illustrate the risk of fragmentation.
- There is broad consensus that multiple networks will endure, each specializing in different use cases; the critical requirement is fast, seamless capital movement between these venues.
- Despite present headwinds in crypto prices and shifting market focus toward traditional value stocks, substantial inflows from institutions are expected if/when regulatory and operational barriers are resolved.

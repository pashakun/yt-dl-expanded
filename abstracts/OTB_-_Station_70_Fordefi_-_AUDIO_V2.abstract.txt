PART 1 — ABSTRACT

This conversation centers on the launch of an industry-first failover wallet solution for disaster recovery in crypto custody, developed via a collaboration between Station 70 and Fortify. The discussion explores how the inability to quickly and securely switch wallet providers during outages or security breaches poses existential risks to institutional crypto holders, with parallels drawn to fail-safes in traditional finance. As mainstream institutions, including banks and broker-dealers, increase their crypto exposure—with limited in-house expertise in advanced cryptography like MPC (multi-party computation)—solving these operational resilience gaps is particularly urgent. However, questions remain about whether such disaster recovery infrastructure will gain wide adoption, how quickly legacy institutions can close the crypto custody expertise gap, and if regulatory and technical complexities might introduce new forms of risk or failure modes.

PART 2 — KEY IDEAS

- Institutional crypto security failures (like Bybit and FTX) often result from basic operational lapses, not just advanced technical attacks, highlighting gaps in “101 level” security hygiene.
- Traditional finance expects—and requires—failover and redundancy, but no comparable disaster recovery tooling has existed for self-custody wallets prior to this solution.
- The failover wallet integration enables clients to rapidly recover and reconstitute wallet access, or sweep funds to uncompromised addresses, in the event of downtime or breach at their primary wallet provider.
- The technical achievement relies on leveraging MPC and zero knowledge processes to transfer control of addresses securely, without exposing key material or disrupting user attribution.
- Regulatory changes and renewed bank interest in crypto custody increase demand for institutional-grade security and resiliency—but few bank teams possess the requisite technical or operational crypto custody expertise.
- Many institutions (even sophisticated ones) have a poor understanding of MPC and private key management; banks will likely need to partner with or smartly acquire crypto-native security firms.
- For deep cold custody (e.g., ETF custody), banks may prefer slower, more manual processes, but anything “always-on” or involving on-chain interaction will require seamless, resilient infrastructure and more advanced policy engines.
- Policy and governance tooling, while currently bundled with MPC wallets, could be valuable even outside crypto, suggesting potential for technological spillover into mainstream enterprise and financial infrastructure.
- Better disaster recovery is becoming “table stakes” as digital assets mature into a mainstream asset class, but unclear whether regulatory pressure or market discipline will drive widespread adoption.
- There are still open operational frictions (e.g., with staked assets or unplanned events) and lingering risks that even sophisticated failover solutions may not prevent all forms of systemic failures or black swans.

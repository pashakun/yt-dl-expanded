## Abstract

PART 1 — ABSTRACT

This conversation centers on Fidelity’s early and ongoing engagement with crypto and digital assets, particularly how a major traditional finance institution adapts to—and shapes—the evolving crypto landscape. It explores the practical, cultural, and regulatory hurdles in bridging traditional finance (TradFi) and decentralized finance (DeFi), focusing on product innovation (like custody and tokenized funds), regulatory clarity, and the challenge of balancing risk and opportunity within a conservative institutional environment. The stakes are high: if institutions like Fidelity lead or stall adoption, it shapes how much of finance moves onto new rails, and at what speed. Many tensions remain unresolved, including the pace and form of legacy system modernization, approaches to regulatory compromise, and the uncertain trajectory of product integration across digital and legacy asset classes.

PART 2 — KEY IDEAS

- Institutional involvement in crypto arose from deliberate curiosity and internal experimentation rather than external pressure or hype.
- Early use cases often failed to materialize, but foundational services like custody and mining proved to be commercially impactful and credibility-enhancing.
- Regulatory clarity (such as the passage of the “Genius Act”) is critical but often arrives late and through contentious negotiation, which can force compromises—like not paying interest on stablecoins.
- Internal debates (e.g., over stablecoins paying interest) highlight a tension between traditional financial values and new business models emerging in crypto.
- Large financial organizations face dilemmas of whether to build, buy, or partner for crypto capabilities, typically reserving build efforts for differentiating technology.
- The modernization of financial infrastructure will be evolutionary—not a wholesale replacement of TradFi by DeFi—constrained by interconnectedness, inertia, and the presence of small, less adaptable players.
- True innovation requires a deliberate culture of risk-taking, internal mobility, and “safe spaces” (labs/incubators) to experiment and tolerate fast failures.
- Product innovation (such as tokenized money market funds interoperable with stablecoins) reflects a strategy to bridge legacy finance with DeFi use cases.
- Bitcoin, though seen as “old-fashioned,” retains a central role as a digital store of value, both institutionally and personally, and will likely remain foundational even as other crypto assets and products emerge.
- The main risks for institutions remain reputational (rather than purely technical or regulatory), making adoption timelines contingent on leadership willingness to tolerate uncertainty and look past short-term discomfort.

PART 1 — ABSTRACT

This conversation centers on the rapid adoption and use of stablecoins as a foundation for savings and payments—both for individuals and businesses—in the Asia-Pacific region, through the lens of Bernardo Bilotta’s company, Stables Money. It highlights the growing real-world utility of stablecoins, not just as speculative crypto assets, but as practical tools for cross-border transactions, remittances, and daily spending, especially in fragmented or underbanked markets. The discussion underscores how regulatory uncertainty and the need for robust compliance and banking partnerships are significant frictions to scale, even as market demand intensifies. At stake is whether stablecoin-driven platforms can sustainably navigate evolving legal regimes, institutional integration, and security risks, while continuing to unlock new financial use cases. Unresolved questions include how quickly regulatory frameworks will evolve, whether banks will transition from skepticism to active collaboration, and how these platforms can balance innovation with risk mitigation.

PART 2 — KEY IDEAS

- The primary user base for stablecoin-based platforms like Stables includes both crypto-native individuals seeking real-world utility and businesses needing efficient cross-border payments; developers seeking integration are a growing demographic.
- Stablecoins meet strong demand in Asia-Pacific due to fragmented currency systems, slow and costly legacy payment rails, and capital controls—conditions not as prevalent in developed Western economies.
- Product adoption has grown organically, driven by solving tangible pain points (e.g., inability to easily access or spend in USD), with little reliance on incentives or marketing.
- Transaction sizes are typically larger than consumer fintech apps, indicating usage for substantive payments, remittances, and possibly business purposes rather than microtransactions.
- Most users do not store idle balances on the platform—velocity of money (spending and on-off ramping) dominates over holding, partially because yield features are absent due to regulatory concerns.
- Regulatory clarity, particularly regarding offering yield or savings features, has lagged, making compliance a priority and limiting some product capabilities.
- Stables has built a robust compliance-first approach, securing licenses across several jurisdictions and investing in KYC/AML, transaction monitoring, and proprietary risk management tools to satisfy both regulators and banking partners.
- Banking integration is a major friction, with banks historically skeptical but increasingly warming to stablecoin platforms, especially as the costs of non-participation come into focus.
- Key risks include compliance failures (especially regarding money laundering or illicit uses), security breaches, and the need to maintain institutional trust.
- There remains significant uncertainty as to how quickly financial institutions, regulators, and users will standardize approaches to stablecoin payments, and whether new business models (such as yield-bearing stablecoin accounts) will gain regulatory approval.

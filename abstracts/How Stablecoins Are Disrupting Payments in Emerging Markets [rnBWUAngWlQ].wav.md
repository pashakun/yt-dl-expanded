## Abstract

PART 1 — ABSTRACT

This conversation centers on how stablecoins are transforming payments and financial infrastructure in emerging markets, focusing on the opportunities and frictions that arise when digital assets interface with traditional banking and cross-border transactions. The discussion highlights why stablecoins are attractive: they solve real problems in regions where access to reliable banking and foreign exchange is costly or slow, often making stablecoin-based payments cheaper and faster than legacy methods. The conversation matters because it illustrates a real shift in global financial flows, where large institutions are cautiously entering the space and legacy rails are being challenged—not in major corridors, but in underserved or high-friction routes. However, open questions remain about regulatory acceptance, the willingness of traditional banks to embrace new risks, the future of CBDCs versus private stablecoins, and whether the efficiencies of crypto-native technology can be fully harnessed given institutional conservatism and compliance demands.

PART 2 — KEY IDEAS

- Stablecoins are already widely used in emerging markets to facilitate cross-border payments, fill gaps in dollar access, and serve as a practical alternative to slow or unreliable correspondent banking, especially in "fragile" currency corridors.
- Traditional banks have been slow to embrace stablecoin innovation, often focusing on tokenizing existing assets but neglecting the fundamental role of cash and payments in digital asset adoption.
- Banking access remains a major friction point: many crypto-native companies and even their customers risk de-banking, making stablecoin settlement more attractive, but also heightening counterparty and regulatory risk.
- Institutional players gain unique advantages when they can bridge reliable digital asset execution with deep banking integrations, delivering fast FX and on/off-ramp services across multiple currencies.
- The major institutional hesitation is regulatory: large banks must balance new business opportunities with strict compliance, long internal approval processes, and aversion to reputational or career risk.
- There is skepticism about the ability of CBDCs to address cross-border needs, given their likely confinement to national rails and lack of open, permissionless infrastructure, in contrast to the practical advantages of stablecoins.
- Launching proprietary bank stablecoins may not be essential; instead, large banks can profitably support external stablecoin issuers by providing banking, custody, FX, and distribution services.
- Big banks see stablecoin-enabled payments as potentially cannibalizing existing business, but early movers can capture significant market share and new revenues by meeting evolving corporate treasury and commodity trading needs.
- The Libra/DM episode showed that both regulatory pushback and inappropriate "tech-first" attitudes can derail stablecoin adoption at scale, though it also educated banks and opened conversations not focused on bitcoin or volatility.
- A significant unresolved tension lies in whether large financial institutions will truly open up to digital asset businesses as clients, or remain gatekeepers, constraining the full benefits stablecoins offer to global commerce.

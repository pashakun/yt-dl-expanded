PART 1 — ABSTRACT

This conversation centers on the rapid evolution of stablecoin infrastructure and its integration into traditional financial institutions. It explores how legacy banks, payment processors, and fintechs are variously positioned—ranging from cautious curiosity to active experimentation—in their engagement with stablecoins, and considers the operational, regulatory, and technical hurdles they face. The discussion highlights both the promise of stablecoins to dramatically improve cross-border payments and treasury management, as well as persistent fragmentation in infrastructure and ambiguity around regulatory treatment, particularly for banks. Core unresolved questions revolve around how quickly regulatory clarity will arrive for stablecoin usage and custody, and how institutions will adapt their technology and risk management frameworks to leverage these digital assets at scale.

PART 2 — KEY IDEAS

- Settlement efficiency through stablecoins can potentially remove the need for banks to maintain nostro/vostro accounts, improving capital efficiency and reducing operational complexity.
- Financial institutions are at widely varying stages of stablecoin adoption, with payment service providers (PSPs) generally more aggressive than traditional banks due to regulatory caution.
- The underlying stablecoin infrastructure exists but remains fragmented, requiring complex integrations for custody, on/off-ramps, token and chain selection, and compliance, which slows institutional adoption.
- Recent regulatory signals (e.g., the US "Genius Act") have increased institutional willingness to consider stablecoin products but leave open questions about balance sheet treatment and permissible activities.
- Advances in blockchain technology—such as high-throughput chains and improved custody options—make stablecoin payments more feasible for institutional use cases compared to just a few years ago.
- There is continued global demand for US dollar stability, especially in volatile markets, which stablecoins make more accessible to both institutions and end-users.
- Ongoing regulatory uncertainty, especially regarding the holding and accounting of stablecoins, makes banks hesitant and often pushes them toward indirect solutions or gray-area workarounds.
- Providers like Tesser aim to reduce onboarding friction by bundling custody, treasury management, multi-chain support, reconciliation, and compliance into a turnkey, white-labeled solution for FIs.
- Institutional trust in crypto payments is rising due to better AML, KYC/KYB practices and increased regulatory oversight, but consistent global standards are still lacking.
- The biggest barriers to institutional stablecoin adoption are less about raw technology and more about fragmented tooling, evolving regulation, and risk management concerns.

# Latest trends in stablecoin adoption and policy with First Digital CEO Vincent Chok

## Abstract

PART 1 — ABSTRACT

This conversation centers on the evolving role and significance of stablecoins—particularly First Digital’s FDUSD—in the digital asset landscape, as well as the regulatory, technological, and institutional dynamics shaping their adoption. It underscores how stablecoins act as critical infrastructure for liquidity, cross-border payments, and financial inclusion, potentially challenging traditional banking rails and facilitating broader access to financial services. The discussion weighs the importance of regulatory frameworks, the benefits of trust-based structures, and the interactions between stablecoins, DeFi, and real-world assets. However, questions remain about optimal regulatory approaches, the management of institutional risk, how existing financial incumbents will adapt or resist, and how to balance innovation with consumer protection and compliance.

PART 2 — KEY IDEAS

- The growth of stablecoins is expected to accelerate, with predictions of the sector surpassing $2 trillion in market cap in the next decade, yet regulatory clarity and institutional trust are still works in progress.
- FDUSD and similar new entrants aim to differentiate through regulatory preparedness, trust-based asset segregation, and compliance infrastructure, positioning themselves to rapidly adapt to new or evolving rules.
- Stablecoins serve not only as payment and settlement tools but also as foundational liquidity for exchanges, OTC desks, and cross-chain protocols, approaching the status of "oxygen" for the crypto ecosystem.
- The market remains highly concentrated among two dominant stablecoins, but alternatives argue there is a need for optionality, competition, and intermediary roles akin to correspondent banking.
- Partnerships with large exchanges (e.g., Binance) are critical for traction and network effects, but velocity of token movement is an under-discussed metric that highlights real usage over raw supply.
- Trust company legal structures are touted for providing dual-balance sheets, asset segregation, and enhanced customer protection, especially in cases of corporate distress or regulatory actions.
- Asia, and Hong Kong specifically, are seen as forward-thinking regulatory environments offering growth opportunities, but the global patchwork of standards and US regulatory influence remains a source of uncertainty and risk.
- The prospect of neo-banking and DeFi-based models leveraging stablecoins raises fundamental questions about the future role of banks, central bank oversight, and the potential for greater financial inclusion, yet could create tensions with existing monetary systems.
- Tokenization of real-world assets (RWA) is rapidly gaining traction, but its success is contingent on functioning stablecoin rails and the development of appropriate compliance and custodial frameworks.
- Education, both for institutional actors and the public (including children), is seen as a key challenge given the prevalence of scams and misinformation, highlighting the need for widespread engagement to foster responsible adoption.

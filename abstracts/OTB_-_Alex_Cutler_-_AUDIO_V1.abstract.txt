PART 1 — ABSTRACT

This conversation is fundamentally about the evolving landscape of decentralized trading, specifically the role of decentralized exchanges (DEXs) such as Aerodrome on Coinbase’s Base blockchain, and their capacity to provide permissionless, transparent, and highly efficient market infrastructure. It matters for the future of crypto and finance because the combination of open markets, new forms of incentives, and deeper liquidity has the potential to reshape how assets—from cryptocurrencies to FX and eventually real-world assets—are traded and accessed globally, potentially bypassing many of the frictions and gatekeepers of traditional finance. The discussion underscores the increasing collaboration between DeFi protocols and large institutions, the potential for on-chain FX to disrupt significant existing markets, and shifting incentive models, but leaves unresolved the issues of mass institutional adoption, regulatory clarity, the challenges of bootstrapping stablecoin liquidity for less liquid currencies, and whether DEXs can sustainably scale to handle all asset classes and regulatory scrutiny.

PART 2 — KEY IDEAS

- Decentralized exchanges (DEXs) create permissionless, transparent, and open marketplaces; anyone can contribute value, regardless of traditional credentials.
- The economic model of Aerodrome aligns incentives across traders, liquidity providers, and token holders, distributing 100% of DEX revenues directly via the protocol token, eliminating privileged access for insiders.
- DEXs achieve rapid market expansion and liquidity provisioning—centralized exchanges (CEXs) cannot match this pace due to regulatory and operational overhead for new listings and asset support.
- CEXs are increasingly integrating or surfacing DEX liquidity, blending advantages of both models and reducing their own burdens in market-making and listings.
- On-chain trading brings radical transparency: all participant actions and liquidity are visible, reducing information asymmetries and reliance on opaque market makers.
- Significant institutional and payment use cases are emerging around on-chain FX, with the promise of better rates, lower fees, and always-on settlement compared to traditional FX rails.
- Main friction for on-chain FX and non-USD stablecoins remains a lack of liquidity and robust on/off-ramp infrastructure; closing these gaps requires both DeFi-native bootstrapping and engagement from local partners or large incumbents.
- Regulatory uncertainty (especially in the U.S.) has been a major constraint but appears to be easing, making institutional entry and experimentation more likely.
- The future competitive advantage for DEXs lies in being the deepest, most liquid backend infrastructure rather than owning the consumer front end, especially as traditional financial players, wallets, fintechs, and new AI agents compete for user interfaces.
- The DEX incentive model has tradeoffs: network effects and liquidity depth sustain incumbency, but the model is capped by the ability to offer 100% of productive revenue to participants—institutions cannot easily outcompete this by simply offering more.
- Despite optimism around DeFi’s trajectory, exogenous risks—macro shocks and regulatory actions—can still create downside risk and volatility for both protocols and participants.

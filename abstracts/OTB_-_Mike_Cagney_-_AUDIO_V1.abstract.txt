PART 1 — ABSTRACT

This conversation centers on the disruptive potential of blockchain technology, stablecoins, and decentralized finance (DeFi) for the financial sector, particularly focusing on how Figure and its Provenance blockchain aim to re-architect core banking functions and market infrastructure. The discussion highlights an impending shift in bank liabilities from traditional deposits to blockchain-based stablecoins, raising existential questions for regional banks and suggesting new models for asset origination, custody, and trading. While the technology offers efficiency, disintermediation, and potential yield advantages, significant regulatory and structural challenges persist—especially regarding best execution rules, the role of incumbents like DTCC, market structure, and how regulatory frameworks will evolve to accommodate or resist decentralized models. The fundamental tension remains between embracing the innovative promise of DeFi-driven infrastructure versus the inertia and vested interests of established financial institutions.

PART 2 — KEY IDEAS

- Blockchain-native infrastructure can make the roles of legacy entities like DTCC, centralized exchanges, and prime brokers obsolete by enabling direct, bilateral asset trading and settlement.
- Regional and super regional banks are at risk of massive deposit flight to interest-bearing stablecoins; the government and regulators may be unable or unwilling to fully protect them.
- Institutional stablecoins (e.g., JP Coin) from the largest banks are likely to outcompete regional bank offerings due to scale and brand, threatening regional banks’ relevance.
- The growth of private credit on blockchains indicates both assets and liabilities are shifting away from traditional banking channels, with DeFi providing a new locus for capital allocation.
- Regulatory frameworks such as best execution/Reg NMS create significant friction for blockchain-native venues to compete with national securities exchanges, requiring workarounds like distinct on-chain CUSIPs.
- There is a paradox in regulators’ and bankers’ attitudes: acknowledgment of the risk and inevitability of deposit flight, yet reluctance to engage with new technological solutions.
- The current regulatory "clarity" conversation is fragmented, with centralized, decentralized, and incumbent interests all seeking different outcomes, and persistent ambiguity over how DeFi fits into legacy structures.
- Technological limitations (e.g., blockchain scalability and privacy for sensitive financial data) are being addressed, but questions remain about whether value accrues at the chain (L1) or application layer.
- Decentralized custody and trading offer efficiency and user control, but require fundamental shifts in compliance, investor protection, and market intermediation models.
- The long-term outcome is uncertain: while major disruption is expected, questions remain about whether value captured by new blockchains will match or eclipse that of the legacy payment and settlement infrastructure, and how consumer surplus will ultimately manifest.

PART 1 — ABSTRACT

This conversation centers on the evolution of Squads—a Solana-based multi-signature wallet—into Altitude, a stablecoin-powered financial platform aimed at addressing the treasury and financial operations needs of global businesses. The discussion explores how stablecoins and blockchain-based financial infrastructure are reshaping traditional banking by enabling programmable, efficient, and instant financial services that can reach underserved markets without heavy reliance on legacy systems or regulatory barriers. This transformation has significant implications for how businesses—both crypto-native and traditional—manage, move, and hedge money internationally, potentially disrupting incumbent banking models and accelerating the migration of financial activity onto blockchain rails. However, persistent questions remain around the scalability of non-USD stablecoins, the extent to which local currencies will be displaced or complemented, regulatory hurdles across jurisdictions, and whether incumbent banks can adapt quickly enough to avoid being sidelined by more agile, crypto-native providers.

PART 2 — KEY IDEAS

- Squads began as a crypto-native, self-custodial treasury tool and evolved into Altitude, targeting broader business financial operations, especially those using or transitioning to stablecoins.
- The current stablecoin and blockchain financial stack remains complex, with many intermediaries; the industry’s long-term goal is direct, end-to-end settlement minimizing legacy rails and fees.
- Altitude leverages stablecoins to offer business accounts, expense management, yield on deposits, and programmable workflows, appealing to both crypto-native and traditional businesses, especially in underserved geographies.
- The platform's expansion strategy relies on bypassing legacy banking constraints—enabling cross-border service without local licenses, driven by the self-custodial design and blockchain rails.
- There is growing demand for multi-currency stablecoin solutions, but USD stablecoins remain dominant due to global dollarization and network effects, though tokenization of other major currencies is seen as inevitable over time.
- Businesses are attracted not only by access to stable store-of-value and yield opportunities but also by instant liquidity and programmable automation unavailable in legacy banking.
- Risks and frictions include regulatory uncertainty, the challenge of building robust non-USD stablecoins, potential pushback from governments concerned about currency flight, and the obstacles to integrating new rails at scale.
- Incumbent banks and neobanks face an innovator’s dilemma; their existing business models rely on payment inefficiencies and float, making it structurally difficult to fully embrace instant, blockchain-based finance.
- Local banks are likely to see market share erode to either large, global institutions or nimble, fintech-native providers, unless they radically embrace new rails and technology.
- There is an expectation that programmable blockchain infrastructure for treasury and inter-company settlements will appeal to enterprises seeking efficiency gains—potentially further disrupting traditional banking relationships.

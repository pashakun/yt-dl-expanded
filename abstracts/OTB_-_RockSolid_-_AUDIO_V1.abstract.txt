PART 1 — ABSTRACT

This conversation centers on RockSolid, a DeFi protocol designed to provide user-friendly “vault” products that enable individuals and institutions to earn yield on crypto assets, with a particular focus on Rocket Pool’s liquid staking token (rETH). The discussion explores how DeFi yield products—specifically vaults—can abstract away the technical and risk management complexities that currently deter non-expert users while enhancing the integration and utility of decentralized staking tokens within the wider crypto ecosystem. The participants debate the challenges of competing with centralized or heavily VC-funded protocols like Lido, and address tradeoffs between decentralization and practical business development. Unresolved questions revolve around whether vaults can attract and sustain organic user adoption without reliance on backroom incentives, how decentralized these processes can truly remain, and what barriers—technical, cultural, or regulatory—might hinder vault-based DeFi from achieving mainstream or institutional scale.

PART 2 — KEY IDEAS

- The adoption and utility of liquid staking tokens (LSTs) in DeFi depend less on nominal yield rates and more on their integration and composability with various DeFi protocols.
- Centralized efforts and aggressive VC funding have positioned Lido’s stETH as the most widely integrated LST, whereas Rocket Pool’s decentralized ethos initially deprioritized business development, limiting its reach.
- User experience barriers in DeFi—like complex yield strategies and risk management—restrict broad adoption; vaults can solve this by packaging strategies into simple, “single-click” products.
- There are inherent tradeoffs: vault operators must make some centralized decisions about strategy deployment and incentives, slightly departing from pure decentralization.
- Much of DeFi’s total value locked (TVL) is driven by institutional or “whale” backroom deals, contradicting the ethos of democratized access, yet vaults can be a mechanism for retail and smaller players to collectively participate in such opportunities.
- Vault structures resemble investment vehicles like ETFs in TradFi by abstracting complexity, but remain open and permissionless, enhancing accessibility—albeit with jurisdictional regulatory restrictions.
- Institutions and digital asset treasuries (DATS) are expected to be a major source of future DeFi capital, since vaults can be tailored to their risk, regulatory, and reporting requirements.
- Regulatory compliance, including exclusion of users from certain jurisdictions (like the US and UK), is an immediate operational concern for scaling vault products.
- The protocol’s economic model relies on a mix of AUM-style fees and performance fees with transparent, smart contract-enforced rules.
- Strategic success for vault providers will be measured both by TVL growth and by the number and quality of integrations and incentive campaigns they can negotiate on behalf of ecosystem assets like rETH.

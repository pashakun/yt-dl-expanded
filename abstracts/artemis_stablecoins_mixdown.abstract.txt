PART 1 — ABSTRACT

This conversation centers on a new, first-of-its-kind survey and data analysis of real-world stablecoin payment usage, aiming to provide a grounded and granular assessment of the actual scale and modalities of stablecoin transactions beyond speculative trading. The significance lies in moving the discourse from abstract, top-line numbers to concrete, bottoms-up data around business and consumer payments, thereby informing stakeholders—including policymakers, financial institutions, and crypto skeptics—about the genuine footprint and utility of stablecoins in global payment flows. The findings expose that, despite headlines about trillions in stablecoin volumes, authentic payment usage remains a minuscule fraction of the total addressable market, yet is growing rapidly. Unresolved issues include the limited coverage of the study (key players absent), the challenge of disaggregating genuine economic activity from non-payment uses, the need for greater transparency, and deeper understanding of end use cases, regional dynamics, and economic impact.

PART 2 — KEY IDEAS

- Most publicized stablecoin transaction volumes are misleadingly inflated by trading and non-payment settlement, with authentic payment activity (B2B, B2C, P2P, card) representing a tiny portion of overall volumes.
- The study estimates current annualized real-world stablecoin payments at roughly $100 billion, less than 0.1% of the global B2B payment market, highlighting both early-stage penetration and substantial future growth potential.
- Recent B2B stablecoin payment volumes have experienced explosive year-over-year growth (approx. 400%), signaling rapid adoption but from a very low base.
- Conservative, aggregated data collection was necessitated by competitive sensitivities and the lack of incentive for firms to share granular information, resulting in undercounting and potential blind spots.
- Established players like Tether overwhelmingly dominate payment volumes (approximately 90%), but regionally there are notable variances in stablecoin and blockchain preference due to local context.
- P2P and remittance data is likely underrepresented, as much on-chain activity occurs outside surveyed intermediaries, making institutional data an incomplete proxy for overall adoption.
- Stablecoins are positioned as transformative “infrastructure” for moving money and potentially other assets (like equities) globally, enabling new forms of financial access, inclusion, and disintermediation.
- Institutional and regulatory actors remain skeptical about actual stablecoin utility; detailed, credible data is foundational for informed policymaking and regulatory development.
- Major gaps persist—including the exclusion of some significant exchanges and insufficient detail on end use cases—which limit the comprehensiveness and actionable insights of the analysis.
- There is an expressed aspiration for ongoing, more comprehensive and granular reporting to resolve outstanding questions and track the evolving impact of stablecoins on the financial system.

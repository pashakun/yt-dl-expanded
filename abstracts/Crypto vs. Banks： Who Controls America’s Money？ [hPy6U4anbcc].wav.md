## Abstract

PART 1 — ABSTRACT

This conversation centers on the competitive dynamics between traditional banks and emerging crypto platforms, particularly in offering financial products such as interest-bearing accounts. The dialogue evaluates whether established financial institutions can or should adapt to the attractive yields provided by crypto alternatives, specifically stablecoins, and whether regulatory approaches encourage or stifle this competition. The discussion raises important questions about how regulatory frameworks may influence innovation, the accessibility of financial services, and whether current systems serve consumer interests or protect incumbents. Unresolved tensions include how to balance consumer protection with fostering innovation, and to what extent traditional banks will adapt or resist the changing landscape.

PART 2 — KEY IDEAS

- Crypto platforms offer significantly higher yields on deposits (e.g., stablecoins) compared to traditional banks.
- Banks and their regulators may resist competition by lobbying for regulatory obstacles or not adapting their offerings.
- A level regulatory playing field is advocated to allow direct competition between banks and crypto providers.
- The persistence of low bank yields reflects both policy and institutional inertia rather than purely market realities.
- Consumer choice and access to high-yield savings are positioned as central to future financial infrastructure.
- If banks offered comparable yields, competitive pressure could improve financial products industry-wide.
- Regulation may either be an enabler or a barrier to the adoption and integration of crypto in mainstream finance.
- Tension exists around whether consumer protection justifies limiting crypto offerings or merely sustains incumbency for banks.

## Abstract

PART 1 — ABSTRACT

The conversation centers on why venture capital firms are increasingly focused on investing in stablecoins and decentralized finance (DeFi), discussing both the current landscape and forward-looking opportunities. This matters for the future of crypto and financial infrastructure, as the success or failure of these technologies will shape which rails underpin global payments and how traditional finance integrates with blockchain innovations. The discussion highlights the competitive dynamics between dominant stablecoin issuers and localized or infrastructure-focused players, as well as the interplay between stablecoins and tokenized bank deposits. Key uncertainties linger around regulatory clarity, user adoption patterns, and whether institutional and retail incumbents or crypto-native upstarts will capture the most value, with particular tension regarding innovation pacing and the risk of sector complacency.

PART 2 — KEY IDEAS

- The global payments sector is being re-architected in real time via stablecoins and blockchain technologies, signaling a foundational transformation for money movement.
- The U.S. stablecoin issuance space is seen as saturated, but local, non-USD (e.g., Colombian peso) stablecoins and infrastructure-as-a-service platforms remain promising investment targets.
- Distribution advantages of incumbent retail platforms (such as Robinhood and SoFi) position them to capture outsized market share as crypto expands to the masses.
- DeFi is evolving through new primitives, especially the growth of decentralized perpetual exchanges (perpetual DEXs), and by abstracting complexity to allow seamless user interaction via mainstream platforms.
- Venture capital offers potential upside over direct token purchases by enabling early entry, deeper diligence, and active portfolio engagement in a fast-moving, constantly changing landscape.
- There is an ongoing debate between stablecoins and tokenized bank deposits, with the view that stablecoins are likely to become the broadly accepted digital currency for external use, while tokenized deposits (e.g., JPM Coin) will remain limited to internal or niche applications.
- The risk of complacency in the crypto sector, particularly reduced innovation intensity, is flagged as a major vulnerability—even amid optimism regarding current founder quality.
- Regulatory uncertainty and institutional adoption remain significant wildcards for the trajectory of both DeFi and stablecoins.
- The maturity and adoption of new DeFi protocols often outpace general investor awareness, creating opportunities for early investors but also increasing risks related to rapid obsolescence or protocol failure.

## Abstract

PART 1 — ABSTRACT

This conversation examines the rapid global growth of self-custody crypto wallets, using Trust Wallet as a primary example, and interrogates what this trend reveals about the shifting landscape of financial control and access. The discussion explores how everyday users—ranging from those with minimal balances to those holding substantial wealth—are adopting self-custody for purposes like remittances, yield generation, and diversification beyond traditional banks. It highlights the evolving user experience and ongoing security challenges, especially the balance between usability and absolute asset control, alongside concerns about personal safety and regulatory approaches. The dialogue underscores key unresolved tensions: how to design wallets that are both simple and genuinely sovereign, what role regulation should play in self-custody, and how the concept might extend to encompass not just digital assets but data and personal identity in an increasingly on-chain world.

PART 2 — KEY IDEAS

- Rising adoption of self-custody wallets is driven by global demand for borderless, low-cost financial tools, notably for remittances and stablecoin transfers between developed and emerging markets.
- User base is highly diverse, including individuals with very small holdings as well as those custodizing millions, indicating broadening appeal and utility.
- Main friction points include user education, onboarding complexity, and the inherent trade-off between ease of use and complete user sovereignty over assets.
- Technical innovations, such as key sharding and encrypted backups, improve experience but generally still involve some degree of compromise on pure self-sovereignty.
- Personal security risks (e.g., kidnappings) are discussed, but the core risk profile is not seen as unique to self-custody; systemic exposure rises as crypto ownership grows.
- Regulatory outlook varies by jurisdiction; while the US currently enshrines self-custody rights, hybrid models or regulatory interventions remain possible in other regions.
- Users are expanding wallet usage from simple holding to more complex activities: swapping, DeFi participation, and holding tokenized real-world assets, challenging the boundaries between banks and crypto-native solutions.
- The role of wallets is fundamentally distinguished from banks: wallets provide user agency and optionality rather than managed financial services, shifting responsibility to individuals.
- Looking forward, self-custody may extend beyond financial assets to include control of data and digital identity, raising questions about ownership, privacy, and the power individuals have over their most valuable resources.
- The future evolution of self-custody is seen as deeply intertwined with societal questions about rights, individual autonomy, and what it means to own or control digital assets in an interconnected, AI-enhanced world.

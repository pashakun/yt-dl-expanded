## Abstract

PART 1 — ABSTRACT

This conversation centers on the readiness of decentralized finance (DeFi) infrastructure to meet the needs and standards of institutional trading, evaluating both recent progress and remaining deficiencies. Its significance lies in DeFi’s potential to transform traditional finance by offering new yield opportunities and operational efficiencies, but also in the persistent frictions regarding accounting standards, risk management, regulatory clarity, and the gap between centralized and decentralized asset management. While innovation is advancing, the discussion underscores unresolved tensions around trust, standardization, capital efficiency, and true decentralization—issues that must be resolved for DeFi to scale safely and sustainably in institutional portfolios.

PART 2 — KEY IDEAS

- The DeFi infrastructure has improved but remains only partly mature; full institutional-grade solutions are still emerging, especially for complex needs.
- Current weak points include institutional accounting (tracking NAV, tax, and custody), which hinders large-scale adoption.
- Institutional players are not yet fully comfortable with replacing established financial intermediaries (like auditors and fund administrators) with on-chain alternatives.
- Many institutional participants access crypto primarily through vehicles like ETFs, rather than engaging natively with DeFi protocols—limiting the sector’s growth and innovation.
- Efficient capital management is lacking: cross-portfolio margin and risk-sharing tools common in traditional finance are largely absent or immature in DeFi.
- There is a notable absence of robust insurance, downside protection, and risk management instruments in DeFi, restricting institutional trust and participation.
- The sector still grapples with the true meaning of decentralization; some leading protocols are substantially centralized in governance and operation, raising concerns for regulators and allocators.
- Clarity in regulation is critical: premature or excessive oversight may stifle innovation, but some oversight is seen as necessary to build trust and safety.
- Market shocks—such as exchange failures or excessive leverage—continue to expose systemic risks; resilience and responsible risk practices remain in development.
- The hope is that, through cycles of excess and correction, stronger, more cautious actors will come to dominate, fostering sustainable and reliable returns.

## Abstract

**PART 1 — ABSTRACT**

The conversation centers on the proposal and rationale for developing a dedicated Layer 1 blockchain specifically tailored for stablecoin transactions. It examines how traditional Layer 1 blockchains introduce friction—primarily unpredictable gas fees and network congestion—that undermines stablecoin efficiency, especially for institutional users who need predictability and rapid settlement. The discussion matters for the future of crypto and financial infrastructure because stablecoins are becoming pivotal in cross-border payments, institutional finance, and emerging markets; how these are managed at the protocol level could define mainstream adoption and integration with existing financial systems. However, open questions remain regarding the long-term dominance of US dollar-based stablecoins, regulatory uncertainties across jurisdictions, market appetite for multiple versus consolidated stablecoin platforms, and the ability of alternative tokens or locally-focused solutions to gain traction against established incumbents.

---

**PART 2 — KEY IDEAS**

- Predictable and low transaction costs are fundamental for stablecoin adoption among institutions; current Layer 1s introduce volatility and unpredictability.
- Using a stablecoin itself (e.g., USDT) as the native gas token eliminates fee volatility and makes network costs transparent.
- Sub-second finality and guaranteed block space subscriptions are technical differentiators aimed at ensuring rapid, predictable settlements for both retail and institutional users.
- Peer-to-peer stablecoin transactions can be rendered entirely gas-free within a purpose-built Layer 1, reducing friction for everyday users.
- Growth in stablecoin usage is occurring on two fronts: institutional flows dominate in value, while retail and emerging markets drive transaction volume.
- Regulatory clarity, such as frameworks recently instituted in the US, is a prerequisite for serious institutional adoption and innovation in stablecoin infrastructure.
- There is uncertainty whether a few large stablecoins (like USDT, USDC) will maintain dominance or if a proliferation of institution-specific or jurisdiction-specific tokens will emerge.
- Jurisdictional fragmentation is likely, with domestic stablecoins arising alongside dominant cross-border options, shaped by local regulation and market needs.
- The overwhelming dominance of US dollar-pegged stablecoins may persist, but there is potential for other fiat-pegged stablecoins, especially once regulatory frameworks allow.
- The underlying risk remains whether new, custom Layer 1s for stablecoins can attract sufficient users and liquidity to succeed against broadly adopted networks and solutions.

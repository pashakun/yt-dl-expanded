## Abstract

PART 1 — ABSTRACT

The discussion centers on the emergence of stablecoin yields and their potential role in redefining the concept of the risk-free rate within crypto and traditional finance. As yield-generating products become standard on digital assets, particularly stablecoins, both crypto platforms and traditional banks are vying to adapt to this evolving landscape, facing regulatory and competitive pressures. This shift matters because it could democratize access to previously exclusive financial strategies, alter the dynamics between centralized and decentralized platforms, and potentially transform the foundation of institutional finance and savings rates globally. However, unresolved tensions persist around regulatory acceptance, standardization of risk disclosures, the centralization of yield sources, and user protection, particularly as new products blend DeFi and CeFi models. The future path for regulatory frameworks, competitive responses from incumbents, and the true definition of "risk-free" in this new context remain open questions.

PART 2 — KEY IDEAS

- Crypto yield products, especially for stablecoins, are expected to become a standard offering in the near future, mirroring traditional banking's structured products.
- Traditional banks and large crypto exchanges are positioned for intense competition as banks may adapt to offer competitive stablecoin-related yields.
- The yield environment is sensitive to macroeconomic shifts; falling treasury yields will drive platforms to seek real-world assets as alternative yield sources.
- Centralized crypto yield platforms are moving towards greater transparency and over-collateralization, aiming to address previous failures and rebuild user trust.
- Tokenization of traditional financial instruments, like treasuries, could anchor a new risk-free rate in the crypto economy, with staking yields from high-profile tokens possibly serving as alternative benchmarks.
- Regulatory scrutiny is increasing, with unresolved debates over whether regulators will clamp down on or ultimately embrace crypto yield products, particularly as incumbent banks lobby against them.
- Offering institutional-grade strategies (e.g., basis trades, private credit) to retail users is democratizing finance, but presents risks related to user sophistication and product complexity.
- The user experience for accessing crypto yields is improving via abstraction and simplification, facilitating broader adoption but potentially obscuring underlying risks.
- Centralization of yield offerings among a few dominant players is identified as a major systemic risk, potentially recreating vulnerabilities similar to traditional finance.
- The boundary between centralized (CeFi) and decentralized (DeFi) platforms is increasingly blurred, as collaborations emerge to deliver varied risk-return profiles to users, raising new questions about transparency and risk allocation.

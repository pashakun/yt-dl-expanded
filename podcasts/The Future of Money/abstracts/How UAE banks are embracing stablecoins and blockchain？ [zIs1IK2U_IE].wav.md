## Abstract

PART 1 — ABSTRACT

This conversation centers on how banks in the UAE, and by extension other jurisdictions, are increasingly adopting stablecoins and blockchain technologies, navigating evolving regulatory landscapes and shifting institutional attitudes towards crypto assets. The discussion highlights the growing legitimacy and utility of stablecoins within mainstream finance—particularly around payments, remittances, and trade finance—while acknowledging persistent risk factors, especially for banks interfacing with both traditional and decentralized systems. It matters because the integration of blockchain and digital assets may reduce cross-border frictions, streamline financial processes, and attract more global market players to progressive hubs like the UAE, potentially reshaping global financial infrastructure. Still, questions remain about regulatory harmonization, risk management (especially regarding liquidity and system downtime), the optimal use of local versus global stablecoins, and the degree to which banks will embrace public versus closed blockchain systems.

PART 2 — KEY IDEAS

- Regulatory shifts, including new acts and changing administrations in the US and GCC, are loosening past prohibitions and making banks more willing to engage with crypto, especially stablecoins.
- The utility of stablecoins extends beyond trading to core financial services like supply chain finance, cross-border payments, and remittances, provided the supporting infrastructure matures.
- Changing risk appetites among correspondent banks may facilitate more global integration, but legacy risk concerns persist, especially with US banks’ exposure to crypto-related institutions.
- Local-currency stablecoins could become increasingly important as liquidity pairs, challenging the dominance of USD-backed tokens and helping to localize crypto markets.
- Banks are likely to first adopt stablecoins and blockchain in closed-loop systems for specific use cases, such as trade finance, due to risk management considerations, before wider public blockchain adoption.
- Real-world, yield-bearing assets (e.g., property, debt, tokenized gold) are emerging as new forms of collateral and investment within digital asset systems, potentially unlocking significant value for banks and clients.
- The adoption of digital asset services by banks is closely tied to their capacity for risk control; higher risk tolerance and robust management frameworks enable deeper crypto engagement.
- Dubai and the UAE seek to position themselves as global crypto innovation hubs by offering clear regulatory frameworks (e.g., VARA licenses), possibly triggering a domino effect among other regional banks.
- AI is being rapidly integrated by forward-looking banks for transactional monitoring, cost optimization, fraud detection, and payment solutions, often in tandem with blockchain initiatives.
- Major operational risks remain, particularly maintaining uninterrupted service in crypto’s 24/7 markets, which poses significant technical and resource challenges for traditionally structured banks.
